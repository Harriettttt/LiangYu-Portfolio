# Does Milnepan Reduce 7-Day Mortality in Heffpox Patients? A Causal Analysis

**Statistical Modelling and Inference for Health - project**

---

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background
Heffpox is a serious disease with high mortality within 7 days of diagnosis. A new treatment, milnepan, has been proposed to reduce this risk. This report examines whether prescribing milnepan **causes** a reduction in 7-day mortality among heffpox patients at Hundred Acre Hospital.

### 1.2 Causal Question
**Primary estimand**: The Average Treatment Effect (ATE) of milnepan on 7-day mortality (death7day) among heffpox patients.

We aim to estimate the causal odds ratio comparing the odds of 7-day mortality if all patients received milnepan versus if none received milnepan.

---

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Study Population
Patients diagnosed with heffpox (heffpox = 1) were included. The final cohort comprised **N = 8,192** patients, of whom **3,948 (48.2%)** received milnepan and **4,244 (51.8%)** did not.

### 2.2 Outcomes
- **Primary outcome**: 7-day mortality (death7day) - binary indicator used for causal inference
- **Secondary outcome**: Time to event (TEVENT) - continuous variable used for exploratory survival analysis

### 2.3 Causal Framework
A Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) was constructed based on clinical knowledge (Figure 2):

**Confounders** (variables affecting both treatment assignment and outcome):
- Age, sex, BMI, smoking status, diabetes

**Mediator** (on the causal pathway, not adjusted):
- ICU admission (occurs after milnepan initiation)

**Effect modifier** (as noted in the brief):
- Diabetes may interfere with milnepan's action

The **minimal sufficient adjustment set** identified from the DAG was: {age, BMI, diabetes, sex, smoking}.

### 2.4 Missing Data Handling
Multiple imputation using chained equations (MICE) was performed with:
- **m = 20** imputed datasets
- **BMI**: Predictive mean matching (PMM) for continuous variable
- **Smoking**: Logistic regression for binary variable
- Results pooled using Rubin's rules

### 2.5 Causal Effect Estimation
Two complementary approaches were used:

1. **Outcome Regression**: Multivariable logistic regression adjusting for all confounders
2. **Inverse Probability of Treatment Weighting (IPTW)**: Propensity score-based weighting to balance confounders

Both methods were applied across all 20 imputed datasets with proper variance estimation.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Missing Data

**Table 1: Missing Data Summary**

| Variable | N Missing | % Missing |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| BMI | 2,686 | 32.8% |
| Smoking | 383 | 4.7% |

All other variables had complete data. The missing data pattern suggested a Missing at Random (MAR) mechanism, justifying the use of multiple imputation.

### 3.2 Baseline Characteristics

**Table 2: Baseline Characteristics by Treatment Group**

| Variable | No Milnepan (n=4,244) | Milnepan (n=3,948) | SMD |
|----------|----------------------|-------------------|-----|
| Age, mean (SD) | 48.5 (20.1) | 39.8 (13.5) | **0.511** |
| BMI, mean (SD) | 27.9 (4.0) | 28.1 (4.0) | 0.041 |
| Male, n (%) | 2,001 (47.1%) | 1,984 (50.3%) | 0.062 |
| Current smoker, n (%) | 1,050 (25.9%) | 863 (23.0%) | 0.069 |
| Diabetes, n (%) | 627 (14.8%) | 225 (5.7%) | **0.303** |

**Key observations**: 
- Patients receiving milnepan were significantly **younger** (SMD = 0.51 > 0.1)
- Patients receiving milnepan had **lower diabetes prevalence** (SMD = 0.30 > 0.1)
- These imbalances indicate confounding by indication that must be addressed

### 3.3 Overall Mortality

The overall 7-day mortality rate was **40.4%** (3,306/8,192 patients).

### 3.4 Exploratory Survival Analysis (TEVENT)

Figure 1 shows Kaplan-Meier survival curves stratified by milnepan treatment.

**Key findings**:
- **Median survival**: 8.47 days (95% CI: 8.05-8.85) in the no-milnepan group; **not reached** in the milnepan group
- **Events**: 2,322 deaths in no-milnepan group vs 1,765 in milnepan group
- **Log-rank test**: p < 2.2×10⁻¹⁶, indicating a statistically significant difference
- The milnepan group consistently showed higher survival probability throughout follow-up

### 3.5 DAG Analysis

Figure 2 presents the assumed causal structure. The DAG identifies:
- **Total effect pathway**: milnepan → death7day (direct) + milnepan → ICU → death7day (indirect via mediator)
- **Adjustment set**: {age, BMI, diabetes, sex, smoking}

ICU was **not** included in adjustment to estimate the **total causal effect**.

### 3.6 Primary Analysis: Causal Effect Estimates

**Table 3: Comparison of Causal Effect Estimates for 7-Day Mortality**

| Method | Odds Ratio | 95% CI | p-value |
|--------|------------|--------|---------|
| Outcome Regression | **0.868** | (0.789 - 0.955) | 0.004 |
| IPTW | **0.861** | (0.785 - 0.944) | 0.002 |

**Interpretation**: 
- Both methods yield **highly consistent** estimates (OR ≈ 0.86-0.87)
- Milnepan is associated with approximately **13-14% reduction** in the odds of 7-day mortality
- The effect is **statistically significant** (p < 0.01 for both methods)

**Other significant predictors from logistic regression**:
- Age: OR = 1.024 (1.021-1.027), p < 0.001 — each year increase raises odds by 2.4%
- BMI: OR = 1.068 (1.052-1.083), p < 0.001 — each unit increase raises odds by 6.8%
- Diabetes: OR = 1.719 (1.480-1.996), p < 0.001 — diabetes increases odds by 72%

Figure 4 displays the forest plot comparing both estimation methods.

### 3.7 Covariate Balance project(IPTW)

Figure 3 shows the Love plot of standardized mean differences before and after IPTW. After propensity score weighting, all covariates achieved adequate balance (SMD < 0.1), supporting the validity of the IPTW estimates.

### 3.8 Sensitivity Analysis: Effect Modification by Diabetes

Given the clinical note that diabetes may interfere with milnepan's mechanism, subgroup analysis was performed:

**Table 4: Subgroup Analysis by Diabetes Status**

| Subgroup | OR | 95% CI | p-value |
|----------|-----|--------|---------|
| **No Diabetes** | **0.750** | (0.678 - 0.829) | <0.001 |
| **Diabetes** | **4.488** | (3.124 - 6.447) | <0.001 |

**Critical finding**: 
- In patients **without diabetes**, milnepan shows a **protective effect** (25% reduction in odds of death)
- In patients **with diabetes**, milnepan appears **harmful** (4.5-fold increase in odds of death)
- This represents **strong effect modification** by diabetes status

---

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Summary of Findings

This analysis provides evidence that milnepan has a **heterogeneous effect** on 7-day mortality in heffpox patients:

1. **Overall effect**: Milnepan reduces 7-day mortality (OR = 0.87, p < 0.01)
2. **Without diabetes**: Strong protective effect (OR = 0.75, p < 0.001)
3. **With diabetes**: Harmful effect (OR = 4.49, p < 0.001)

### 4.2 Consistency of Causal Methods

The two estimation approaches produced remarkably consistent results:
- Outcome regression: OR = 0.868
- IPTW: OR = 0.861

This consistency strengthens confidence in the causal interpretation.

### 4.3 Clinical Implications

**Recommendation for Dr. C. Robin**:
1. Milnepan appears effective in reducing 7-day mortality for heffpox patients **without diabetes**
2. Milnepan should be used with **extreme caution** (or avoided) in patients **with diabetes**, as it may increase mortality risk
3. Diabetes status should be a key consideration in treatment decisions

### 4.4 Assumptions and Limitations

**Causal assumptions**:
1. **No unmeasured confounding**: We assume all confounders are captured in the DAG
2. **Positivity**: Both treatment groups exist across covariate strata
3. **Consistency**: Treatment is well-defined
4. **MAR assumption**: Missing data depends only on observed variables

**Limitations**:
- Observational design limits causal certainty compared to randomized trials
- High BMI missingness (33%) may introduce bias despite imputation
- The diabetes interaction finding requires validation in independent data

---

## 5. Conclusion

Based on this causal analysis of 8,192 heffpox patients:

1. **Milnepan reduces 7-day mortality overall** (OR = 0.87, 95% CI: 0.79-0.95)
2. **The effect is strongly modified by diabetes status**:
   - Beneficial in non-diabetic patients (OR = 0.75)
   - Potentially harmful in diabetic patients (OR = 4.49)
3. **Clinical recommendation**: Milnepan should be prescribed to heffpox patients without diabetes. For diabetic patients, alternative treatments should be considered.

## Appendix A: Statistical Methods Details

### A.1 Multiple Imputation
- **Software**: mice package in R
- **Number of imputations**: m = 20
- **Methods**: 
  - BMI (continuous): Predictive mean matching (PMM)
  - Smoking (binary): Logistic regression
- **Iterations**: 10 per imputation
- **Pooling**: Rubin's rules for combining estimates and variances

### A.2 Propensity Score Model
- **Treatment**: milnepan (binary)
- **Covariates**: age, sex, BMI, smoking, diabetes
- **Estimand**: Average Treatment Effect (ATE)
- **Weight truncation**: 1st and 99th percentiles (double-sided) to reduce extreme weights
- **Variance estimation**: Survey-weighted regression (svyglm) for robust standard errors

### A.3 Causal Assumptions
1. **Conditional exchangeability**: No unmeasured confounders given adjustment set
2. **Positivity**: 0 < P(milnepan=1|X) < 1 for all X
3. **Consistency**: Observed outcome = potential outcome under received treatment
4. **No interference**: One patient's treatment does not affect another's outcome
